Learning Concept

Logistic Regression transforms a linear equation into a probability using the Sigmoid function, squashing outputs between 0 and 1.

The Decision Threshold dictates the cutoff. Lowering the threshold captures more positives (increases Recall) but also catches more false alarms (lowers Precision). This is the fundamental tradeoff in classification!

Learning Concept

Linear Regression models the relationship between variables by fitting a linear equation (y = mx + b) to observed data.

Mean Squared Error (MSE) measures the average squared difference between estimated and actual values (the red dashed residuals).

Gradient Descent calculates the derivative of the error curve and iteratively updates the slope and intercept, taking small steps downwards to find the global minimum.

Learning Concept

KNN is a "lazy" algorithm—it doesn't learn mathematical weights. It memorizes the training data and classifies new points based on Euclidean proximity.

The choice of K is crucial: A small K (e.g., K=1) is highly sensitive to noise and outliers (Overfitting). A large K creates smoother boundaries but might blur classes together.

Learning Concept

SVM aims to find the optimal hyperplane that creates the "widest street" (maximum margin) separating classes.

The Support Vectors (highlighted with yellow rings) are the critical data points lying exactly on or inside the boundaries of this street. They alone dictate the model!

The C Parameter dictates tolerance for errors. High C creates a strict, narrow margin (risk of overfitting). Low C creates a soft, wide margin (better generalization).

Polynomial Features allow models to fit non-linear data by artificially adding x², x³, etc.

Degree 1 is too simple (High Bias / Underfitting). High degrees (10+) bend wildly to touch every training point, learning pure noise (High Variance / Overfitting). The goal is the minimum point on the Validation Error curve.

Learning Concept

Regularization prevents overfitting by mathematically penalizing large coefficients during training.

L1 (Lasso) creates a sharp diamond-shaped mathematical constraint. This naturally forces some coefficients to hit exactly zero, acting as automatic Feature Selection.

L2 (Ridge) creates a smooth circular constraint. It shrinks all coefficients asymptotically towards zero but never eliminates them. It is highly effective at stabilizing Multicollinearity.

Learning Concept

MAE (Mean Absolute Error): The average absolute distance between predictions and actuals. It is robust to outliers and easy to interpret in business terms.

MSE (Mean Squared Error): Squares the errors before averaging. This severely penalizes large mistakes (visualized by the purple squares).

R² (R-Squared): Represents the proportion of variance explained by the model. 1.0 is a perfect fit, 0.0 means the model is as good as guessing the mean.
